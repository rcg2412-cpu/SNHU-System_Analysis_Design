# CS 255 DriverPass System Analysis and Design

## Project Summary

DriverPass is a company that wanted to improve how students prepare for their driver's license exams. The goal of the project was to design a cloud-based system that combines online training with behind-the-wheel driving instruction. Customers need to be able to create accounts, purchase training packages, take practice tests, track their progress, and schedule driving lessons. The system also needs to give DriverPass employees tools to manage customers, appointments, instructors, vehicles, and user accounts.

## What did I do particularly well?

I think one of the strongest parts of my work was turning the client's requirements into system models that show how the different parts of DriverPass work together. For example, the scheduling design checks instructor and vehicle availability before creating a reservation, while the online training design shows how students access training materials and have their progress recorded. Creating several types of UML diagrams also helped me look at the same system from different perspectives instead of trying to represent everything in one diagram.

## What would I revise?

If I could revise one part of my work, I would improve the original requirements document by making some of the functional and nonfunctional requirements more specific. Some of the early requirements described what the system needed to accomplish but could have included clearer conditions for how those requirements would be measured or tested. I would also connect the requirements more directly to the later UML diagrams so it would be easier to trace a client requirement from the original interview to the final design.

## How did I interpret the user's needs?

I started by identifying what each type of user actually needed to accomplish with DriverPass. Students need a simple way to access training, track their progress, and manage driving appointments. Employees need to manage customers and reservations, while administrators need additional control over accounts and system information. I used those needs to determine the system functions and then modeled how they interact. Considering the user's needs is important because a system can work technically and still fail if it does not solve the problems users actually have or is difficult for them to use.

## How do I approach designing software?

My approach is to understand the requirements before deciding how the software should be structured. I first identify the users, what they need from the system, and the information the system needs to manage. From there, I break larger processes into smaller workflows and use diagrams to understand how the pieces connect. In the future, I would continue using techniques such as requirements analysis, use cases, activity diagrams, sequence diagrams, and class diagrams before moving into development. I would also review the models against the original requirements throughout the design process so that changes do not cause important client needs to be overlooked.
